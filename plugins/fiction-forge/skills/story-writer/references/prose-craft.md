# Prose Craft Reference

## Sentence-Level Craft

### Concrete vs. Abstract
Instead of: "The room was messy"
Write: "Takeout containers covered the desk, and a shirt hung from the back of the only chair that wasn't stacked with books"

### Active vs. Passive Voice
Active (preferred for action): "She kicked the door open"
Passive (for deliberate effect): "The door was kicked open" (emphasizes the door, hides the actor)

### Strong Verbs
Replace weak verb + adverb with a strong verb:
- "walked quickly" → "strode" or "hurried"
- "said loudly" → "shouted" or "called"
- "looked carefully" → "examined" or "scrutinized"
But don't overdo it — sometimes "walked" is exactly right.

### Filtering
Remove unnecessary filter words that distance the reader from the POV character:
- Instead of: "She saw the door open" → "The door opened"
- Instead of: "He heard footsteps" → "Footsteps echoed down the hall"
- Instead of: "She felt the cold air" → "Cold air bit her skin"
Exception: Use filter words deliberately to emphasize perception or uncertainty.

## Paragraph-Level Craft

### Opening Lines
Strong chapter/scene openers:
- Action: Drop into the middle of something happening
- Image: A striking visual that sets tone
- Voice: A distinctive thought or observation from the POV character
- Tension: Something is wrong, and the reader wants to know what

Weak openers to avoid (unless intentional):
- Weather descriptions
- Character waking up
- Describing the room/setting in exhaustive detail
- Backstory

### Paragraph Rhythm
- Short paragraphs: urgency, emphasis, impact
- Long paragraphs: immersion, complexity, flowing thought
- One-line paragraphs: maximum punch (use sparingly)
- Mix all three for rhythm

## Scene-Level Craft

### Scene Openings
- Establish time, place, POV quickly (1-3 sentences)
- Start as late in the scene as possible
- Give the reader something to be curious about

### Scene Transitions
- Hard scene breaks (###) for jumps in time/place/POV
- Soft transitions within scenes for time passage
- Match the transition style to the genre and pacing

### Scene Endings
- End on a turn (the value shifted)
- Hook: question, revelation, tension, or quiet dread
- Cut the scene as soon as the turn happens — don't linger

## Dialogue Craft

### Distinct Voices
Give each character their own speech pattern:
- Vocabulary level (educated, colloquial, archaic, technical)
- Sentence length (terse, verbose, balanced)
- Verbal tics or habits (pet phrases, filler words, formal address)
- Contractions (uses them freely, avoids them, specific patterns)
- Topic preferences (what they talk about, what they avoid)

### Dialogue Beats
Break up dialogue with action beats showing what characters DO:
"I don't know what you're talking about." She straightened the papers on her desk, aligning each edge precisely. "You must have me confused with someone else."

### Subtext
Characters should rarely say exactly what they mean:
- A character saying "I'm fine" while their hands are shaking
- Arguing about dishes when the real fight is about trust
- Polite conversation where every word is a weapon
