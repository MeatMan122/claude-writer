# Prose Authenticator

Your purpose is to ensure fiction prose reads as if written by a skilled human author, not generated by AI. Apply these checks during writing and as a revision pass.

## Two-Pass Architecture

This skill is used in two contexts:
1. **During composition**: The story-writer skill loads this AND the humanizer skill, internalizing the rules while drafting. The goal is to write clean prose from the start.
2. **During audit**: The prose-refiner agent loads this AND the humanizer skill on the finished draft as a fresh pair of eyes. The goal is to catch what slipped through.

Both passes use the same rules but from different vantage points: writer internalization vs. cold detection.

## Layer 0: Humanizer Pass

Before running the fiction-specific layers below, apply the humanizer skill's 25 AI-writing patterns. Load `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` for the comprehensive checklist covering:
- Content patterns (significance inflation, promotional language, vague attributions, formulaic structures)
- Language patterns (AI vocabulary, copula avoidance, negative parallelisms, synonym cycling)
- Style patterns (em dash overuse, boldface, emojis, curly quotes)
- Communication patterns (chatbot artifacts, sycophantic tone)
- Filler and hedging (filler phrases, excessive hedging, generic conclusions)

The humanizer handles general AI-pattern removal. The layers below handle fiction-specific craft.

## Layer 1: Anti-AI-ism Detection

### Vocabulary Blacklist
Flag and replace these overused AI words/phrases:
- "delve" / "delving" → dig into, explore, examine, investigate
- "tapestry" (as metaphor) → web, network, fabric, texture
- "intricate" → complex, layered, detailed, tangled
- "nuanced" → subtle, layered, complex, shaded
- "embark" → start, begin, set out, launch
- "beacon" → signal, guide, light, sign
- "labyrinth" / "labyrinthine" → maze, tangle, warren, web
- "testament" → proof, evidence, sign, demonstration
- "multifaceted" → complex, varied, layered
- "paradigm" → model, framework, pattern, system
- "landscape" (non-literal) → field, domain, scene, terrain
- "pivotal" → crucial, key, decisive, turning-point
- "realm" (non-fantasy) → area, world, domain, sphere
- "foster" → build, grow, nurture, cultivate
- "endeavor" → attempt, effort, try, pursuit
- "resonate" → connect, ring true, hit home, echo
- "underscore" → highlight, emphasize, stress, reveal

### Structural AI Tells
Flag these patterns:
- Paragraphs that start with "In a world where..." or "It was a [adjective] [noun]..."
- Paragraphs where every sentence is roughly the same length
- Excessive use of three-part lists ("the [X], the [Y], and the [Z]")
- Transitions between every paragraph (Moreover, Furthermore, However)
- The pattern: statement → elaboration → conclusion in every paragraph
- Purple prose: overwrought descriptions stacked with adjectives and metaphors
- Melodramatic emotional descriptions: "A wave of [emotion] crashed over [character]"
- Filter words in tight POV: "She realized," "He noticed," "She felt"
- **"Not X, but Y" constructions**: "Not fear — concentration." AI overuses this negation-then-correction structure. Once per chapter maximum. Show instead of negate-then-state
- **Aphoristic non-truths**: Lines that sound quotable but aren't actually true on examination. "Instruments lie" — no they don't. If a line performs wisdom, verify it's actually wise and specific to the character thinking it

### Hedging in Narrative Voice
Flag wishy-washy narrative:
- "seemed to," "appeared to," "was somewhat," "was rather"
- "almost as if," "as though perhaps," "in a way"
- Narrative voice should be confident. Uncertainty belongs to characters, not narrators.

## Layer 2: Prose Rhythm

### Sentence Length Variation
Check that sentences vary in length:
- A passage of 5+ sentences all between 15-25 words is a red flag
- Good rhythm: a mix of short (5-10 words), medium (10-20), and long (20-35) sentences
- Short sentences for impact: "She stopped." "The door was open." "He was gone."
- Long sentences for immersion and complexity

### Sentence Opener Variety
Check that not all sentences start with:
- The subject ("He," "She," "The," "It")
- Participle phrases ("Walking into the room," "Turning to face him,")
Good variety includes: action first, dependent clause first, dialogue first, setting detail first

### Paragraph Length Variety
Check for varied paragraph lengths:
- Not all paragraphs should be 3-5 sentences
- Single-sentence paragraphs for impact
- Longer paragraphs for immersive description or complex thought
- The rhythm should feel organic, not mechanical

## Layer 3: Dialogue Authenticity

### Character Voice Distinctness
For each character who speaks in the chapter:
- Compare their dialogue against their Voice Profile in the appendix
- Check: Are they using their established speech patterns?
- Check: Could you tell who's speaking without the dialogue tag?
- Flag if two characters sound identical

### Narrative Voice Distinctness (across POV characters)
If the story uses rotating POV, check that the narrative voice changes with the POV character:
- Does the narrative notice different things when filtered through different characters?
- Does sentence rhythm change between POV characters?
- Does vocabulary level or register shift?
- If two chapters with different POV characters have identical narrative voice, that's a flag. Each POV should be unmistakably theirs — not just in dialogue, but in how the narration itself sounds.

### Dialogue Naturalness
- Check for contractions (most modern characters use them in speech)
- Check for incomplete sentences (people don't always speak in full sentences)
- Check for interruptions and overlapping dialogue in tense scenes
- Flag overly formal or stilted speech that doesn't match the character
- Flag exposition dumps disguised as dialogue

### Said Bookism Check
Flag excessive use of: exclaimed, declared, retorted, proclaimed, queried, opined, interjected, averred, articulated, enunciated, vocalized
These should be replaced with "said," "asked," or action beats.

## Layer 4: Show-Don't-Tell

### Emotion Tells
Flag direct emotion statements in dramatic scenes:
- "He was angry" → Show through clenched fists, sharp words, physical tension
- "She felt sad" → Show through behavior, what she notices, body language
- "They were scared" → Show through physiological response, avoidance, hypervigilance

Note: Telling is fine for transitions and low-stakes moments. Don't flag every instance.

### Exposition Tells
Flag passages that explain rather than dramatize:
- "She was the kind of person who..." → Show through action and dialogue
- "The city was known for..." → Weave details into the scene naturally
- Backstory blocks of 3+ paragraphs → Break up, distribute, dramatize

## Layer 5: Voice and POV Consistency

### POV Discipline
In limited third-person or first-person POV:
- The narrator can only know what the POV character knows
- The narrator can only observe what the POV character would notice
- Descriptions should be filtered through the POV character's personality
- Flag any "head-hopping" (switching POV mid-scene without a break)

### Narrative Tense Consistency
- If past tense, flag accidental present tense (and vice versa)
- Dialogue is always present tense regardless of narrative tense

### Tone Consistency
- The overall tone should remain consistent with the established style
- If the story has been darkly humorous, a sudden shift to earnest melodrama is a flag
- Genre tone should be maintained: noir stays noir, literary stays literary

## Application

### During Writing
When the story-writer skill is drafting a chapter, these principles should be applied in real-time during composition. Don't write a draft full of AI tells and then fix them — write authentically from the start.

### As a Revision Pass
When run as a standalone pass on an existing chapter, produce a report:
```markdown
# Prose Authenticity Report: Chapter [N]

## AI-ism Flags
- Line [N]: "delve" → suggest "dig into" or "explore"
- Paragraph [N]: All sentences 18-22 words. Vary length.

## Rhythm Issues
- Section [location]: 4 consecutive paragraphs start with "He..."
- Scene 2: Sentence length monotonous (all medium-length)

## Dialogue Flags
- [Character]'s dialogue in scene 3 sounds identical to [Other Character]
- Line [N]: "he exclaimed vehemently" → "he said" or action beat

## Show-Don't-Tell Flags
- Paragraph [N]: "She felt a deep sorrow" → show through behavior

## POV/Voice Issues
- Paragraph [N]: Narrator knows something POV character shouldn't
```
