# McKee: The Principle of Antagonism
<!-- Source: Robert McKee, "Story" — Chapter 14. The four levels of antagonistic force and value declension. -->

## The Core Principle

McKee considers this the most important and least understood principle in story
design: **A protagonist and story can only be as intellectually fascinating and
emotionally compelling as the forces of antagonism make them.**

Human nature is conservative -- we never do more than we must. Therefore, only
powerful and complex forces of antagonism will cause a protagonist to become
fully realized, multidimensional, and deeply empathetic. The answer to "how do I
make my protagonist compelling?" lies on the negative side of the story.

## The Four Levels of Antagonistic Force

For any positive value at stake in a story, there are four levels of opposing
force, arranged from weakest to strongest:

**Level 1 -- POSITIVE (The Value Itself)**
The starting state or the ideal the protagonist strives toward.

**Level 2 -- CONTRARY**
A somewhat negative situation, but not the full opposite. It is the partial,
moderate form of opposition.

**Level 3 -- CONTRADICTORY**
The direct opposite of the positive value. Fully negative.

**Level 4 -- NEGATION OF THE NEGATION**
A compound negative that is qualitatively worse than the contradictory. It is
the darkest expression of the negative force -- the absolute limit of human
experience for that value. It often involves a perverse form of the positive
masquerading within the negative.

## Value Declension Examples

| Value     | Positive       | Contrary          | Contradictory | Negation of Negation         |
|-----------|----------------|--------------------|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Justice   | Justice        | Unfairness         | Injustice     | Tyranny ("might makes right") |
| Love      | Love           | Indifference       | Hate          | Self-hate                     |
| Love (v2) | Love           | Indifference       | Hate          | Hatred masquerading as love   |
| Truth     | Truth          | White lies/half-truths | Lies      | Self-deception                |
| Life      | Consciousness  | Unconsciousness    | Death         | Damnation                     |
| Wealth    | Rich           | Middle-class       | Poor/suffering| Rich but suffering like poor  |
| Communication | Communication | Alienation     | Isolation     | Insanity                      |
| Freedom   | Freedom        | Restraint          | Slavery       | Slavery perceived as freedom  |
| Courage   | Bravery        | Fear               | Cowardice     | Cowardice perceived as courage|
| Loyalty   | Loyalty        | Split allegiance   | Betrayal      | Self-betrayal                 |
| Maturity  | Maturity       | Childishness       | Immaturity    | Immaturity perceived as maturity |
| Success   | Success        | Compromise         | Failure       | Selling out                   |
| Wisdom    | Wisdom         | Ignorance          | Stupidity     | Stupidity perceived as intelligence |

## Why the Negation of the Negation Matters

The Negation of the Negation is what separates good stories from great ones.

- If a story stops at the Contradictory value (love/hate, justice/injustice),
  it echoes the hundreds of mediocre stories produced every year.
- Stories that reach the Negation of the Negation touch the limit of human
  experience and achieve sublimity.
- The Negation of the Negation is often the most disturbing because it involves
  a perversion -- the negative force wearing the mask of the positive.

## Progression Through the Levels

Generally, stories progress from the Positive to the Contrary in Act One, to
the Contradictory in later acts, and finally to the Negation of the Negation in
the last act -- either ending tragically or going back to the Positive with a
profound difference.

## Diagnosing Weak Stories with Antagonism

When a story feels weak, the inevitable cause is that forces of antagonism are
weak. The diagnostic process:

1. Identify the positive values at stake and their progression.
2. Ask: Which value is preeminent and turns the Story Climax?
3. Ask: Do the forces of antagonism explore all shades of negativity?
4. Ask: Do they reach the power of the Negation of the Negation at some point?
5. Rather than trying to invent likable protagonist traits, build the negative
   side -- the positive dimensions will emerge naturally as a chain reaction.

**Checklist: Antagonism depth**
- [ ] Have you identified the primary value at stake in your story?
- [ ] Have you mapped all four levels of the value declension?
- [ ] Does your story progress through the Contrary and Contradictory?
- [ ] Does it reach (or at least touch) the Negation of the Negation?
- [ ] Is the Negation of the Negation qualitatively different, not just
      quantitatively worse?
- [ ] Do the forces of antagonism feel overwhelming enough to fully test the
      protagonist?
