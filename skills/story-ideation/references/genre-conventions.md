# Genre Quick Reference for Ideation

## What to Establish Per Genre

### Fantasy
Must establish: Magic system (or lack thereof), political/social structure, geography, cultural norms, naming style
Key question: "What makes YOUR fantasy world different from Tolkien/Martin/Sanderson?"
Typical protagonist: Outsider who discovers hidden world/power, reluctant hero, young person coming of age
Typical conflict: Good vs. evil, freedom vs. tyranny, nature vs. civilization, tradition vs. change

### Science Fiction
Must establish: Level of technology, how it changed society, scientific premise, timeline (near-future vs. far)
Key question: "What one change from our world drives everything else?"
Typical protagonist: Explorer, scientist, survivor, rebel, AI/non-human
Typical conflict: Humanity vs. technology, exploration vs. exploitation, freedom vs. control

### Mystery
Must establish: The crime, the detective figure, the setting, the pool of suspects
Key question: "What's the puzzle the reader is solving alongside the protagonist?"
Typical protagonist: Detective (professional or amateur), journalist, ordinary person thrust into mystery
Typical conflict: Truth vs. deception, justice vs. law, safety vs. knowledge

### Thriller
Must establish: The threat, the stakes, the ticking clock, the protagonist's vulnerability
Key question: "What's the worst thing that could happen, and how close does it come to happening?"
Typical protagonist: Ordinary person in extraordinary danger, expert with a flaw, hunted person
Typical conflict: Survival vs. threat, trust vs. betrayal, duty vs. self-preservation

### Romance
Must establish: The two leads, what attracts them, what keeps them apart, the emotional journey
Key question: "Why can't these two people just be together? What has to change?"
Typical protagonist: Two leads with complementary wounds/needs
Typical conflict: Internal barriers (fear, past trauma) and external barriers (circumstances, rivals)

### Horror
Must establish: The source of dread, the rules of the threat, the victims' vulnerabilities
Key question: "What primal fear does this tap into?"
Typical protagonist: Ordinary person, isolated person, skeptic confronting the impossible
Typical conflict: Safety vs. danger, sanity vs. madness, known vs. unknown

### Literary Fiction
Must establish: The protagonist's inner world, the thematic concern, the lens of observation
Key question: "What aspect of the human experience are you illuminating?"
Typical protagonist: Complex, flawed, introspective person at a crossroads
Typical conflict: Internal transformation, relationship dynamics, confronting truth
