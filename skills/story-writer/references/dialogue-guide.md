# Dialogue Guide

## The Cardinal Rule
Dialogue is not conversation. Real conversation is full of "ums," tangents, and banality. Story dialogue is compressed, purposeful, and revealing — it SOUNDS natural while being highly crafted.

## Functions of Dialogue
Every line of dialogue should serve at least one purpose:
1. **Reveal character** — show who this person is through how they speak
2. **Advance plot** — provide or respond to information that moves the story
3. **Create conflict** — characters talking at cross purposes, withholding, manipulating
4. **Establish tone** — set the emotional temperature of the scene
5. **Deliver exposition** — but ONLY when it's natural for the character to say it

If a line serves none of these, cut it.

## Dialogue Tags
- "Said" is invisible. Readers barely register it. Use it freely.
- "Asked" for questions is fine.
- Avoid: "exclaimed," "declared," "retorted," "proclaimed," "queried," "opined," "interjected" — these draw attention to themselves.
- Exception: "whispered," "shouted," "muttered" are fine when they convey necessary volume information.
- Best practice: use action beats instead of tags when possible.

## Multi-Character Conversations
- Give each speaker a distinct rhythm so the reader can track who's talking
- Don't need a tag for every line if the back-and-forth is clear
- Use action beats to ground speakers in physical space
- Break up long group conversations with movement and action
- Not every character needs to speak in every group scene

## Period and Genre Dialogue
- Historical: suggest period without being impenetrable. A flavor, not a dictionary.
- Fantasy: don't default to faux-medieval. Match the world you built.
- Sci-fi: technical jargon should be used naturally, not explained.
- Modern: capture how people actually talk — incomplete sentences, interruptions.

## Common Dialogue Problems
- **On the nose**: Characters say exactly what they think and feel. Fix with subtext.
- **Exposition dump**: Characters explain things they both already know. Fix by finding a character who genuinely needs to learn.
- **Identical voices**: All characters sound the same. Fix by giving each a distinct pattern.
- **Too much dialogue**: Talking heads with no action or setting. Fix with beats and grounding.
- **Too clever**: Every line is a zinger. Fix by letting some lines be simple and functional.
