# Authenticity Techniques

## How Humans Actually Write

### Selective Attention
Real writers don't describe everything. They describe what the CHARACTER notices, filtered through the character's state of mind. An anxious character notices exits and threats. A grieving character notices absence. A hungry character notices food.

### Imperfect Prose (Deliberate)
Not every sentence needs to be elegant. Sometimes a blunt, functional sentence is exactly right:
"He went inside. The house was cold. He turned on a lamp."
This isn't lazy writing — it's controlled pace and tone.

### Emotional Restraint
The most powerful emotional moments are often the most restrained in language. Instead of elaborate descriptions of grief, a character might just notice their coffee has gone cold. Understatement > Overstatement.

### Specific Over General
"A 1987 Toyota Corolla with a cracked windshield" > "an old car"
"She ordered a double espresso" > "she ordered coffee"
"The Glock 19 felt heavier than it should" > "the gun felt heavy"
Specificity creates reality.

### Rhythm as Meaning
Short sentences speed things up. They create urgency. Tension. Fear.

Longer sentences slow things down, allowing the reader to sink into the moment, to feel the texture of the scene, to breathe with the character as they take in their surroundings and let their thoughts unspool.

The rhythm IS the meaning. Don't just vary rhythm for variety — vary it for effect.

### White Space
Not everything needs to be on the page. What's left unsaid is as powerful as what's said. Gaps between dialogue lines, scene breaks at crucial moments, unanswered questions — these create space for the reader's imagination.

## Techniques for Breaking AI Patterns

### Fragment Sentences
Use fragments. Not as a gimmick. As punctuation for thought.
"The door. Right there. Unlocked."

### Mid-Sentence Breaks
Characters don't always finish their—
Or their thoughts get interrupted by action.

### Asymmetric Description
Describe one thing in vivid detail and skip past three others. This creates the feeling of a human noticing what catches their attention.

### Contradictory Sensory Details
"The air smelled like fresh bread and motor oil." Contradictions feel real because reality is messy.

### Echoes and Repetition (Intentional)
Repeat a word or phrase for emphasis:
"She wanted to go home. Home, where the walls didn't close in. Home, where she could lock the door. But home was a thousand miles south and six years past."

### Silence in Dialogue
Characters don't always respond. Sometimes the most powerful response is no response at all. Use "He said nothing" or just describe what the character does instead of speaking.
